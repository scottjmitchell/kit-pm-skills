# Communication Style: Weekly Update

## Purpose

The weekly update is a structured check-in with your manager (Katie Swett Miller, CPO) delivered via Lattice. It serves three functions:

1. **Accountability** — Document what shipped, what's in progress, what's blocked
2. **Visibility** — Keep leadership informed on feature area progress without needing sync meetings
3. **Unblocking** — Surface genuine roadblocks early so Katie can help

This isn't a performance review or a comprehensive activity log. It's a quick snapshot of the week that matters: outcomes delivered, expectations for next week, and any real blockers. Katie should be able to skim it in 2 minutes and know exactly where things stand.

## Format Rules

### Structure

The review has six sections. Only two are required — the rest appear only if there's genuine content.

**Required:**

1. **What's going well** — Wins and measurable impact (3-5 bullets max)
   - Lead with the most meaningful win, not chronologically
   - Focus on outcomes: shipped features, unblocked teams, decisions made, measurable progress
   - Not a list of everything you did — just what moved the needle

2. **Align on expectations** — 1-3 most important things for upcoming week
   - Be specific about what "done" looks like
   - These should be the things Katie would ask about next week
   - 1-3 items only — more suggests lack of focus

**Optional (use only if genuine):**

3. **Challenges or roadblocks** — Real blockers where Katie can help
   - Be specific about what help looks like ("Need design bandwidth for X" not "Design is slow")
   - Don't manufacture problems to fill the section

4. **What I learned** — Genuine learning moments only
   - New insight that changed your thinking or approach
   - Skip if it was just a normal week

5. **How you can support me** — Specific, constructive asks
   - What would make your job easier or help you move faster
   - Must be actionable

6. **Anything else** — Miscellaneous items that don't fit elsewhere
   - Rarely needed

**Length guidance:**
- Total review should be skimmable in 2 minutes
- Most weeks: 5-8 bullet points across all sections
- Quiet weeks can be shorter — that's fine
- A review with just "What's going well" and "Align on expectations" is perfectly acceptable

**Prioritisation:**
- Not everything you did matters for this update
- Ask: Would Katie ask about this if we were grabbing coffee?
- Focus on shipped work, decisions made, and blockers that need escalation
- Skip: routine tasks, internal meetings, day-to-day maintenance

### Voice & Tone

- **Conversational but structured** — Sounds like you're giving a verbal update, not filing a report
- **Honest and direct** — Call wins what they are, call blockers what they are
- **Not performative** — Don't oversell or pad weak weeks with filler
- **Confident about wins** — Own what shipped without false modesty
- **Specific about challenges** — "Waiting on design for app store redesign" not "Some design dependencies"
- **Should sound like you dashed it off** — Polished enough to be clear, not so polished it feels laboured

**Tonal balance:**
- Good weeks: Straightforward confidence, no bragging
- Quiet weeks: Matter-of-fact about in-progress work, no apologising
- Blocked weeks: Clear about the blocker, specific about what would help

### Word Choice

**Lead with outcomes, not activities:**
- "Shipped free plan app access — unblocks 40% of creators from using integrations"
- Not: "Completed final testing and deployed app access feature to production"

**Be specific, not vague:**
- "Finalise automation folders PRD by Thursday, share with engineering for spike planning"
- Not: "Continue working on automation folders"

**Frame blockers as asks, not complaints:**
- "Waiting on design for app store redesign — need mockups by Friday to keep launch on track"
- Not: "Design team is really behind and it's blocking me"

**Include numbers when they matter:**
- "Custom field webhooks shipped — 12 app partners were waiting on this"
- "Completed 5 user interviews for sequence scheduling research"
- Not: "Attended 5 meetings this week"

**Avoid:**
- Corporate jargon: "synergise", "circle back", "touch base"
- Hedging language: "I think maybe we might be able to..."
- False urgency: "CRITICAL" unless it actually is
- Filler in optional sections

**Embrace:**
- Direct verbs: shipped, aligned, decided, unblocked, finalised
- Specific nouns: feature names, team names, partner names, dates
- British English: finalise not finalize, organised not organized

### Common Mistakes

1. **Activity lists instead of outcomes**
   - Not: "Attended app partner sync, reviewed webhook PRD, met with engineering"
   - Better: "Aligned app partners on new webhook capabilities — 3 partners ready to integrate on launch"

2. **Vague next-week goals**
   - Not: "Continue working on automation folders PRD"
   - Better: "Finalise automation folders PRD, incorporate engineering feedback, kick off technical spike"

3. **Including optional sections with filler**
   - If you have nothing genuine for "What I learned", skip it entirely

4. **Burying the lead**
   - Put your biggest win first in "What's going well"

5. **Being too formal or corporate**
   - Not: "Successfully executed delivery of webhooks feature to production environment"
   - Better: "Shipped custom field webhooks — 12 app partners were waiting on this"

6. **Apologising for quiet weeks**
   - Not: "Not much shipped this week, mostly just planning"
   - Better: "Automation folders PRD in review with engineering. Running user interviews for sequence scheduling."

7. **Including blockers that aren't actually blocking you**
   - Only mention blockers if they're preventing you from making progress

## Examples

### Example 1 — Strong Week with Shipped Work

**Input:** Shipped free plan app access. Custom field webhooks went live. Started PRD for automation folders. Blocker: waiting on design for app store redesign. Next week: finalise automation folders PRD, kick off API rate limiting spike.

**Output:**

**What's going well**
- Shipped custom field webhooks — 12 app partners were waiting on this, unblocks integration work for Zapier, Make, and 10 others
- Free plan app access went live — unlocks integrations for 40% of creators who were previously blocked
- Automation folders PRD first draft complete, shared with engineering for early feedback

**Align on expectations**
- Finalise automation folders PRD by Thursday, incorporate engineering feedback, kick off technical spike
- Start API rate limiting discovery with Rohan — goal is to have scope defined for Q2 planning

**Challenges or roadblocks**
- Waiting on design mockups for app store redesign — need by Friday to keep March launch on track

### Example 2 — Quieter Week, Mostly In-Progress

**Input:** Automation folders PRD in review with engineering. Running user interviews for sequence scheduling. Aligned with Rohan on app partner onboarding process. Next week: incorporate engineering feedback on PRD, complete interview synthesis.

**Output:**

**What's going well**
- Aligned with Rohan on app partner onboarding flow — he's building the new process, should cut onboarding time in half
- Completed 3 of 5 user interviews for sequence scheduling research — patterns emerging around timezone handling and send window preferences
- Automation folders PRD in review with engineering, technical feedback due Monday

**Align on expectations**
- Incorporate engineering feedback on automation folders PRD, finalise scope for Q2
- Complete final 2 interviews and synthesise sequence scheduling findings — will inform PRD next week
